\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Draft Manuscript for Research Proposal}}

\begin{abstract}
Distributionally robust data-enabled predictive control (DR-DeePC) provides a promising framework for controlling unknown stochastic linear time-invariant systems directly from input-output data while explicitly accounting for distributional ambiguity and output safety constraints. A critical design parameter in Wasserstein-based DR-DeePC is the ambiguity radius, which governs the trade-off between out-of-sample robustness and closed-loop control performance. Existing approaches typically rely on conservative theoretical bounds or manual tuning, limiting their practical deployment in time-varying and safety-critical settings. This paper proposes an adaptive Wasserstein radius selection framework for DR-DeePC. The method combines an offline data-driven initialization step with an online radius adaptation law driven by prediction mismatch, constraint stress, and distribution shift indicators. A safeguarded projection mechanism is incorporated to prevent the radius from shrinking below a reliability-preserving threshold. The resulting controller preserves the tractable structure of DR-DeePC while improving adaptability to changes in uncertainty statistics. We outline the problem formulation, the adaptive update mechanism, and the expected evaluation methodology. The proposed framework aims to reduce tuning effort and improve the performance-safety trade-off of DR-DeePC under nonstationary uncertainty. A nonlinear quadcopter benchmark is used only as an empirical stress test in the experimental section, not as the domain of validity of the DeePC parametrization.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Data-enabled predictive control, distributionally robust optimization, Wasserstein ambiguity set, adaptive robustness tuning, chance-constrained control.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Model predictive control (MPC) is one of the most successful frameworks for constrained optimal control because it optimizes future control actions using explicit predictions of system behavior while handling state and input constraints in a systematic manner. However, classical MPC depends heavily on the availability of an accurate predictive model. In many real-world applications, including robotics, power electronics, transportation, and energy systems, deriving and maintaining a sufficiently accurate model can be difficult, expensive, or even infeasible. These challenges have motivated the rapid development of data-driven predictive control methods.

Among such methods, data-enabled predictive control (DeePC) is particularly attractive because it avoids explicit model identification and directly uses historical input-output trajectories to synthesize future control actions. The key insight behind DeePC is that for a controllable linear time-invariant system, sufficiently rich historical data can be organized into a trajectory subspace from which future trajectories can be predicted. This allows DeePC to mimic model predictive control behavior while bypassing an explicit parametric model.

Despite its elegance, DeePC becomes significantly more challenging in stochastic settings. In practice, measured data are corrupted by noise, the true disturbance distribution is unknown, and constraint satisfaction must be addressed under uncertainty. Distributionally robust DeePC addresses this issue by replacing a single estimated disturbance distribution with an ambiguity set of plausible distributions. In the Wasserstein-based formulation, the ambiguity set is chosen as a Wasserstein ball centered at the empirical distribution of the collected data. This yields a tractable optimization problem with probabilistic out-of-sample guarantees and explicit robustness against sample mismatch.

A central but unresolved issue in Wasserstein DR-DeePC is the choice of the ambiguity radius. The radius determines the size of the uncertainty set. A radius that is too small may lead to optimistic designs that underperform when the true distribution differs from the empirical one. A radius that is too large can induce severe conservatism, resulting in degraded tracking performance, reduced control authority, and unnecessary constraint margins. Existing practice often relies on either theoretical concentration bounds, which are frequently too conservative for control applications, or empirical grid search, which is computationally expensive and unsuitable for online deployment.

This paper focuses on the adaptive selection of the Wasserstein ambiguity radius in distributionally robust DeePC. Throughout the theoretical development, we restrict attention to the stochastic LTI setting in which the standard DeePC trajectory parametrization is justified by the behavioral systems framework. The motivation is that uncertainty statistics may vary over time, and therefore a fixed ambiguity radius is unlikely to remain appropriate throughout closed-loop operation. Instead, the ambiguity radius should increase when model mismatch, distribution shift, or constraint stress become more pronounced, and it should decrease when the system returns to nominal behavior in order to recover performance.

The main idea of this work is to introduce a two-layer architecture. The inner layer solves a standard Wasserstein DR-DeePC problem for a given radius. The outer layer adjusts the radius online using signals computed from recent closed-loop data. These signals include prediction mismatch, output constraint stress, and a measure of discrepancy between recent observations and the offline data distribution. To prevent unsafe underestimation of uncertainty, the online adaptation is combined with a safeguarded lower bound calibrated offline. This preserves the computational tractability of DR-DeePC while making the robustness level responsive to operating conditions.

The contributions of the proposed study are summarized as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We formulate the Wasserstein radius selection problem for DR-DeePC as an adaptive closed-loop robustness tuning problem rather than a static offline hyperparameter search problem.
    \item We propose a practical online radius update mechanism driven by measurable performance and safety indicators.
    \item We introduce a safeguarded adaptation scheme that combines offline calibration with online adjustment to avoid excessive optimism.
    \item We provide a structured experimental plan to evaluate fixed-radius and adaptive-radius DR-DeePC under stationary and nonstationary uncertainty scenarios.
\end{enumerate}

Section~\ref{sec:experiments} summarizes the current experimental protocol and the main observations obtained so far.

\section{Method Framework}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Formal Setup}
We consider an unknown discrete-time stochastic linear time-invariant system
\begin{equation}
x_{t+1} = A x_t + B u_t + E w_t, \qquad y_t = C x_t + D u_t + F v_t,
\end{equation}
where $u_t \in \mathbb{R}^{m}$ is the input, $y_t \in \mathbb{R}^{p}$ is the measured output, and $w_t,v_t$ are process and measurement disturbances. The matrices $(A,B,C,D,E,F)$ are unknown to the controller. Instead of identifying them explicitly, the controller relies on offline input-output data collected from repeated experiments.

\begin{assumption}
\label{ass:lti}
The underlying plant is controllable and time invariant, and the offline input is persistently exciting of sufficiently high order so that the standard DeePC trajectory parametrization is well defined over the horizon pair $(T_{\mathrm{ini}},T_f)$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark}
Assumption~\ref{ass:lti} is the standard behavioral-systems assumption behind DeePC. It is precisely this LTI setting that justifies the use of the trajectory coefficient vector $g$ and the data matrices $(U_p,Y_p,U_f,Y_f)$. In contrast, the nonlinear quadcopter considered later is used only as an experimental benchmark to test empirical robustness of the controller design.
\end{remark}

Let $u^{(i)}_{0:T-1}$ and $y^{(i)}_{0:T-1}$ denote the $i$th offline trajectory, $i \in \{1,\dots,N\}$. For a chosen initialization length $T_{\mathrm{ini}}$ and prediction horizon $T_f$, these trajectories are arranged into DeePC data matrices
\begin{equation}
U_p,\; Y_p,\; U_f,\; Y_f,
\end{equation}
where the past blocks $(U_p,Y_p)$ encode the initialization window and the future blocks $(U_f,Y_f)$ encode prediction trajectories. Under Assumption~\ref{ass:lti}, a candidate future trajectory is represented by a coefficient vector $g$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
u_{\mathrm{ini}} = U_p g,\qquad y_{\mathrm{ini}} \approx Y_p g,\qquad
u = U_f g,\qquad y = Y_f g.
\end{equation}

The distributionally robust extension replaces a single empirical disturbance law by an ambiguity set. Let $\hat{\mathbb{P}}_N$ be the empirical distribution induced by the repeated offline data and define the Wasserstein ambiguity set
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon}
:=
\left\{
\mathbb{Q}\; \middle| \;
W(\mathbb{Q},\hat{\mathbb{P}}_N)\le \epsilon
\right\},
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon \ge 0$ is the ambiguity radius and $W(\cdot,\cdot)$ denotes the Wasserstein metric associated with a chosen ground norm.

\subsection{Control Objective}
The control objective is to regulate the output toward a reference trajectory while preserving input and output safety constraints under distributional uncertainty. Let
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{U}:=\{u \in \mathbb{R}^{mT_f}\mid \underline{u}\le u\le \overline{u}\},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{Y}:=\{y \in \mathbb{R}^{pT_f}\mid \underline{y}\le y\le \overline{y}\},
\end{equation}
denote the admissible input and output sets over the prediction horizon. For a reference output sequence $y^{\mathrm{ref}}$, we consider a quadratic tracking-and-effort objective of the form
\begin{equation}
\ell(u,y)
:=
\|y-y^{\mathrm{ref}}\|_{Q}^{2}
 + \|u-u^{\mathrm{ref}}\|_{R}^{2},
\end{equation}
where $Q\succeq 0$ and $R\succ 0$ are weighting matrices and $u^{\mathrm{ref}}$ denotes a nominal input around the hovering or equilibrium operating condition.

The design goal addressed in this paper can be summarized as follows: choose an ambiguity-radius sequence $\{\epsilon_t\}_{t\ge0}$ so that the resulting closed-loop controller preserves the convex tractability and safety-aware structure of DR-DeePC while improving the closed-loop performance-risk trade-off under nonstationary uncertainty.

\begin{assumption}
\label{ass:compact}
The sets $\mathcal{U}$ and $\mathcal{Y}$ are nonempty, compact, and convex, and the reference signals remain bounded.
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark}
Assumption~\ref{ass:compact} is standard in constrained predictive control and ensures that the stage cost and constraint functions are evaluated on compact domains. In the present study, the box constraints used in the numerical section satisfy this assumption directly.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Inner DR-DeePC Problem}
At time $t$, given the most recent input-output window $(u_{\mathrm{ini},t},y_{\mathrm{ini},t})$ and a fixed ambiguity radius $\epsilon_t$, the inner control problem is written abstractly as
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:drdeepc-abstract}
\begin{align}
J_t^\star(\epsilon_t)
:=
\min_{g,u,y,\sigma}\quad
& \sup_{\mathbb{Q}\in\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon_t}}
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}} \big[ \ell(u,y) \big]
 + \lambda_{\sigma}\|\sigma\|_1
\label{eq:drdeepc-abstract-cost}
\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
& u_{\mathrm{ini},t} = U_p g,
\\
& y_{\mathrm{ini},t} = Y_p g + \sigma,
\\
& u = U_f g,\qquad y = Y_f g,
\\
& u \in \mathcal{U}, 
\\
& \sup_{\mathbb{Q}\in\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon_t}}
\mathrm{CVaR}_{\alpha}^{\mathbb{Q}}\!\big(h(y)\big) \le 0.
\label{eq:drdeepc-abstract-risk}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Here $\ell(u,y)$ denotes the finite-horizon stage cost, $\sigma$ is the output-consistency slack introduced to accommodate noisy initialization data, and $h(y)\le 0$ represents the output safety constraints. In the tractable Wasserstein DR-DeePC reformulation, the worst-case expectation and worst-case CVaR terms induce regularization and margin terms that remain convex under standard assumptions.

\begin{assumption}
\label{ass:lipschitz}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:compact}, the stage cost $\ell(u,y)$ and the constraint function $h(y)$ are convex and Lipschitz on the compact sets of interest, and the tractable Wasserstein DR-DeePC reformulation of \eqref{eq:drdeepc-abstract} is feasible for all $\epsilon \in [\epsilon_{\min},\epsilon_{\max}]$ considered by the adaptive law.
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark}
Assumption~\ref{ass:lipschitz} is standard in Wasserstein distributionally robust optimization. It excludes pathological cases in which the ambiguity radius is varied over regions where the inner DR-DeePC problem loses convexity or feasibility for trivial reasons unrelated to the adaptive mechanism itself.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Offline Radius Initialization}
The first step of the proposed framework is to determine an initial ambiguity radius $\epsilon_0$ from data. Instead of relying only on theoretical concentration bounds, we propose to use a data-driven calibration procedure over a candidate set $\mathcal{E}$. Typical choices include logarithmically spaced values such as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E} = \{10^{-4}, 2\times 10^{-4}, 5\times 10^{-4}, \ldots, 10^{-1}\}.
\end{equation}

For each candidate radius, a DR-DeePC controller is constructed using a training subset of the offline dataset and evaluated on a validation subset. The validation score should reflect both performance and safety, for example
\begin{equation}
S(\epsilon) = J_{\mathrm{track}}(\epsilon) + \lambda_1 V_{\mathrm{rate}}(\epsilon) + \lambda_2 V_{\mathrm{cvar}}(\epsilon),
\end{equation}
where $J_{\mathrm{track}}$ is the tracking error, $V_{\mathrm{rate}}$ is the empirical constraint violation frequency, and $V_{\mathrm{cvar}}$ is a tail-risk surrogate associated with the observed violations. The best initial radius is then chosen as
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_0 = \arg\min_{\epsilon \in \mathcal{E}} S(\epsilon).
\end{equation}
This offline step produces a radius that is tuned for the available data and serves as the starting point for the online adaptation law.

\subsection{Adaptive Outer-Layer Update}
A fixed ambiguity radius is unlikely to remain optimal when the uncertainty distribution changes over time. To address this issue, we propose to adapt $\epsilon_t$ online using a small set of closed-loop indicators computed over a recent time window.

The first indicator is the prediction mismatch
\begin{equation}
\bar e_t = \frac{1}{W}\sum_{k=t-W+1}^{t} \lVert y_k - \hat y_k \rVert,
\end{equation}
where $\hat y_k$ is the one-step or receding-horizon prediction generated by the DeePC controller. A sustained increase in prediction mismatch suggests that the current ambiguity set may be too small.

The second indicator is the constraint stress
\begin{equation}
\bar s_t = \frac{1}{W}\sum_{k=t-W+1}^{t} \max\{0, h(y_k)\},
\end{equation}
where $h(y_k) \le 0$ denotes the output safety condition. This quantity captures how strongly the closed-loop trajectory is approaching or violating the desired safe set.

The third indicator is a distribution shift estimate. Let $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ref}}$ denote a reference dataset derived from offline trajectories, and let $\mathcal{D}_t$ denote a recent online data window. A discrepancy statistic $\bar d_t$ may be computed using a low-dimensional feature map or a sample Wasserstein distance surrogate between these two windows. An increase in $\bar d_t$ suggests that current operating conditions are no longer well represented by the offline training distribution.

These three indicators are combined into an update law of the form
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_{t+1} = \Pi_{[\epsilon_{\min},\epsilon_{\max}]}\Big(
\epsilon_t + \eta_1(\bar e_t - e_{\mathrm{ref}})
+ \eta_2(\bar s_t - s_{\mathrm{ref}})
+ \eta_3(\bar d_t - d_{\mathrm{ref}})
\Big),
\end{equation}
where $\Pi$ denotes projection onto a prescribed interval, $\eta_i > 0$ are adaptation gains, and $e_{\mathrm{ref}}, s_{\mathrm{ref}}, d_{\mathrm{ref}}$ are nominal reference levels estimated from offline data or an initial calibration phase. This law enlarges the ambiguity radius when the controller observes evidence of mismatch or increased risk, and it reduces the radius when the system behaves consistently with nominal expectations.

\subsection{Safeguarded Adaptation Mechanism}
A fully unconstrained online adaptation law may shrink the ambiguity radius too aggressively and compromise safety. We therefore include a safeguard mechanism.

First, the projection interval is selected so that
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_{\min} \ge \epsilon_{\mathrm{safe}},
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon_{\mathrm{safe}}$ is a conservatively calibrated lower bound obtained from offline reliability analysis, bootstrap calibration, or a conservative cross-validation procedure.

Second, we use asymmetric adaptation gains to react quickly to risk increases and slowly to nominal recovery. Specifically,
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_{t+1}=
\begin{cases}
\Pi(\epsilon_t + \eta_{+} \Delta_t), & \Delta_t > 0, \\
\Pi(\epsilon_t + \eta_{-} \Delta_t), & \Delta_t \le 0,
\end{cases}
\qquad \eta_{+} > \eta_{-} > 0,
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_t$ is the aggregated update signal. This prevents oscillatory or overly optimistic radius reduction.

Third, the radius need not be updated at every time step. A slower update clock can be used so that the outer adaptation loop evolves on a longer time scale than the inner control loop. This reduces sensitivity to noise and helps maintain numerical stability.

\subsection{Basic Analytical Properties}
The purpose of this subsection is not to claim a full closed-loop stability theory yet, but to formalize several structural properties that justify the adaptive mechanism and align the manuscript with a standard control-theoretic presentation.

\begin{lemma}[Radius Projection Invariance]
\label{lem:invariance}
Suppose the update law is implemented as
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_{t+1}
=
\Pi_{[\epsilon_{\min},\epsilon_{\max}]}
\big(\epsilon_t + \delta_t\big)
\end{equation}
for an arbitrary scalar update increment $\delta_t$. If $\epsilon_0 \in [\epsilon_{\min},\epsilon_{\max}]$, then $\epsilon_t \in [\epsilon_{\min},\epsilon_{\max}]$ for all $t \ge 0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The claim follows immediately from the definition of the Euclidean projection operator onto the closed interval $[\epsilon_{\min},\epsilon_{\max}]$. If $\epsilon_t$ belongs to this interval, then its projected update also belongs to the same interval. Since $\epsilon_0$ belongs to the interval by construction, induction completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Boundedness of the Adaptive-Radius Sequence]
\label{prop:bounded}
Under Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance}, the adaptive ambiguity-radius sequence is bounded, and every accumulation point of $\{\epsilon_t\}$ belongs to the compact set $[\epsilon_{\min},\epsilon_{\max}]$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance}, the entire sequence lies in the compact interval $[\epsilon_{\min},\epsilon_{\max}]$. Hence it is bounded and admits convergent subsequences whose limits remain in the same interval.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Monotonicity of the Worst-Case Objective]
\label{lem:objective-monotone}
Let $J_t^\star(\epsilon)$ denote the optimal value of the inner problem \eqref{eq:drdeepc-abstract} at time $t$ for fixed initialization data. Under Assumption~\ref{ass:lipschitz}, if $0 \le \epsilon_1 \le \epsilon_2$, then
\begin{equation}
J_t^\star(\epsilon_1)\le J_t^\star(\epsilon_2).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $\epsilon_1 \le \epsilon_2$, then $\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon_1}\subseteq \mathcal{P}_{\epsilon_2}$. Therefore, for every feasible decision tuple $(g,u,y,\sigma)$, the worst-case expectation over $\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon_1}$ is upper-bounded by the corresponding worst-case expectation over $\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon_2}$. The same inclusion argument applies to the worst-case CVaR constraint. Minimizing over the same feasible decision variables preserves the inequality for the optimal value.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Tractability Preservation Under Radius Adaptation]
\label{prop:tractability}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:lipschitz}, if the inner Wasserstein DR-DeePC problem admits a convex tractable reformulation for every fixed $\epsilon \in [\epsilon_{\min},\epsilon_{\max}]$, then the adaptive-radius controller preserves this tractability at every time step.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The outer layer updates only the scalar parameter $\epsilon_t$ and does not alter the decision variables, the convex stage cost, or the structure of the risk constraints. By Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance}, the updated radius always remains in the admissible interval. Hence each inner problem instance is simply another member of the same convex parametric family, and tractability is preserved.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Lemma~\ref{lem:objective-monotone} formalizes the usual robustness-performance trade-off: increasing the ambiguity radius enlarges the adversarial distribution set and therefore cannot improve the optimistic objective value of the inner problem. This observation motivates the adaptive law: larger radii should only be invoked when empirical evidence indicates increased mismatch or risk.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Noiseless Reduction to Fixed-Radius DR-DeePC]
\label{prop:fixed-reduction}
If the aggregate update signal satisfies $\Delta_t = 0$ for all $t$ and $\epsilon_0 = \bar \epsilon$, then the proposed controller reduces exactly to a fixed-radius DR-DeePC controller with ambiguity radius $\bar \epsilon$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $\Delta_t = 0$ for all $t$, then the update law yields $\epsilon_{t+1}=\Pi_{[\epsilon_{\min},\epsilon_{\max}]}(\epsilon_t)$. Since $\epsilon_t$ already belongs to the admissible interval, the projection acts as the identity map and $\epsilon_{t+1}=\epsilon_t$ for all $t$. With $\epsilon_0=\bar \epsilon$, the sequence remains constant and the inner problem solved at each time coincides with the fixed-radius DR-DeePC controller of radius $\bar\epsilon$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Expected Properties and Evaluation}
The proposed framework is designed to preserve the tractable structure of DR-DeePC because only the ambiguity radius is updated between optimization steps, while the inner problem formulation remains unchanged. The expected benefits are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Reduced manual tuning effort compared with fixed-radius methods.
    \item Improved tracking performance under nominal conditions through controlled radius reduction.
    \item Improved safety robustness under disturbance changes, noise bursts, or distribution shifts through radius enlargement.
    \item Better performance-safety trade-off than fixed conservative radii.
\end{enumerate}

To evaluate the method, we will compare the proposed adaptive-radius DR-DeePC against three baselines: a theoretically sized fixed radius, a manually tuned fixed radius, and a cross-validated fixed radius. Closed-loop experiments should include nominal scenarios, variance shifts, abrupt disturbance changes, and drifting noise statistics. The main evaluation metrics will be tracking error, empirical violation rate, CVaR-like tail violation score, computational time, and the trajectory of the adaptive radius itself.

\section{Experimental Setup and Current Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Paper-Style Evaluation Setup}
Our current evaluation protocol follows the structure of the reference DR-DeePC study as closely as possible within the present software stack. The theoretical controller design remains tied to the stochastic LTI DeePC formulation of Section~\ref{sec:method}. For experimental validation, however, we also use a high-fidelity nonlinear quadcopter-style benchmark as an empirical stress test, following the spirit of the ETH Zurich reference study. The controller is tested in a receding-horizon setting with prediction horizon $T_f = 25$, initialization window $T_{\mathrm{ini}} = 6$, simulation length $250$ steps, and repeated offline experiments of length $15500$. Unless otherwise stated, the Page matrix representation is used with $N=10$ offline batches, output box constraints $[-0.6,1.6]^3$, and Wasserstein nominal radius $\epsilon=0.003$. The inner DR-DeePC problem is solved using an SCS-based conic formulation with warm-started repeated solves.

The current implementation already supports the same core sensitivity studies that motivate the proposed adaptive-radius extension: nominal radius sweeps, batch-count comparisons, horizon/matrix comparisons, and $T_{\mathrm{ini}}$ sweeps. These studies are important because they quantify how sensitive Wasserstein DR-DeePC is to fixed radius selection and how strongly the behavior depends on data richness and trajectory embedding choices.

\subsection{Fixed-Radius Baseline Observations}
The full-scale nominal radius sweep already shows a clear performance-safety trade-off. For example, a very small fixed radius $\epsilon=10^{-4}$ yields tracking error $127.31$ but a high violation rate of $0.784$, whereas a more conservative fixed radius $\epsilon=0.02$ reduces the violation rate to $0.396$ and yields tracking error $118.20$. These preliminary results confirm the practical importance of radius selection and support the claim that a single fixed ambiguity radius may be inadequate across operating regimes.

\subsection{Preliminary Adaptive-Radius Results}
The adaptive-radius study has already produced a first set of full-scale nonstationary comparisons. Under the noise-jump scenario, the adaptive controller achieves tracking error $144.48$, violation rate $0.400$, and tail violation score $0.2387$, while the aggressive fixed-radius baseline with $\epsilon=10^{-4}$ yields tracking error $161.46$, violation rate $0.784$, and tail violation score $0.3882$. A moderate fixed radius $\epsilon=0.02$ reduces the violation rate relative to the aggressive baseline, but still remains less safe than the adaptive controller, with tracking error $138.24$, violation rate $0.480$, and tail violation score $0.2813$. The intermediate fixed-radius case $\epsilon=0.003$ performs particularly poorly in the current run, with tracking error $185.99$ and violation rate $0.780$. These results reinforce that a nominally reasonable fixed radius may still fail to generalize well under disturbance changes.

\subsection{Computational Note on Large Hankel Instances}
One difficult case remains the full-scale Hankel representation with offline horizon $15407$. In the current implementation, this case is solvable for a one-step full-scale closed-loop evaluation, but the computational burden is extremely high, on the order of several hundred seconds per step. We therefore treat this setting as a computational stress case rather than a blocking dependency for the adaptive-radius study. This observation is itself informative, since it highlights a practical scalability limitation of large Hankel embeddings in distributionally robust DeePC.

\subsection{Discussion}
The current experiments support three preliminary conclusions. First, fixed-radius DR-DeePC remains highly sensitive to the ambiguity radius even when the offline dataset and the prediction horizon are held fixed. Second, the benefit of repeated offline batches is clearly visible in the nominal comparisons, which supports the original paper's motivation for distributional robustness with repeated trajectory data. Third, the adaptive-radius controller consistently improves safety-related metrics in nonstationary scenarios, especially when compared against small and moderately tuned fixed radii.

At the same time, the experiments also reveal an important nuance: a relatively conservative fixed radius such as $\epsilon=0.02$ can sometimes match or slightly outperform the adaptive controller in tracking error, but it still yields higher violation frequency and tail-risk than the adaptive scheme. This is precisely the trade-off targeted by the proposed method. Rather than committing in advance to a uniformly conservative design, the adaptive controller attempts to move the operating point online between the optimistic and conservative extremes.

\section{Concluding Remark for the Draft}
This draft outlines a practical and research-relevant extension of Wasserstein distributionally robust DeePC. The proposed direction does not alter the inner DeePC structure; instead, it equips the controller with an adaptive mechanism for selecting the ambiguity radius based on observed closed-loop behavior. This makes the resulting controller more suitable for real operating environments where uncertainty statistics are not stationary.

The current state of the study is already sufficient to support a coherent paper narrative: the fixed-radius baseline has been characterized through nominal, batch-count, and initialization-window studies; the adaptive controller has been evaluated in multiple nonstationary scenarios; and the manuscript now contains a formal problem statement, analytical lemmas, and paper-style figures. The remaining experimental refinements mainly concern richer trace-level visualization and polishing, rather than a missing conceptual core. From this point onward, the writing can transition naturally from method development into full paper assembly, including bibliography integration, figure cross-referencing, and tightening of the theoretical claims.
